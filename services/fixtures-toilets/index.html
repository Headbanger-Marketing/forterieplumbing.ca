Questions About the Work

Can I see the worn part from my toilet or faucet?

A running toilet or dripping tap may trace to a small part, such as a fill valve, flapper or cartridge, which can be kept or photographed for you.

  • Establish which part is faulty first

    Before a fixture is replaced, the respondent should establish which part is faulty and show it to you, whether that is a fill valve, a cartridge, a supply line or a seal. Give the fixture model from its label or tank lid, a photo of the stop valve beneath it and a note of when the fault shows.

  • Compare one part with a full swap

    Compare replacing the single worn part with replacing the whole toilet or tap, and ask what the removed piece or the model label shows about parts still being available. Evidence of a cracked bowl or damaged finish points to a different choice than a worn seal.

  • Name the part found faulty in writing

    If a swap is agreed, ask that the written note names the fixture model removed, the part found faulty and whether it was kept for you to view, with the new model and any connection changed at your Fort Erie home. A loose flange or soft floor noticed while the fixture is off should be listed separately, not added to the agreed work.
